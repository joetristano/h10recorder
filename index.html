Zones are percentages of max HR: Z1 50–60%, Z2 60–70%, Z3 70–80%, Z4 80–90%, Z5 90%+. Entering your age sets max HR with the Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age); you can overwrite it with a measured value. Bars show each zone’s share of total time, including time below Z1.

Edwards load = Σ minutes in zone × weight (Z1=1 … Z5=5, below Z1=0). 0–21 score = 21×(1−e^(−Edwards/150)) — our mapping, not WHOOP's. Banister TRIMP = duration(min) × ΔHR × 0.64×e^(1.92×ΔHR), ΔHR=(avg HR−resting HR)/(max HR−resting HR); resting HR is the value you enter, else the lowest HR seen. HR-based strain under-reads strength work.

Estimate only — not a medical measurement. HR-ratio (Uth et al.): VO₂max ≈ 15.3 × HRmax / HRrest. Cooper 12-min: (distance m − 504.9) / 44.73. 1.5-mile: 483 / time(min) + 3.5. Enter resting HR above, or pick a run test. Accuracy is roughly ±10–15% at best; a lab test is the reference.

Direct BLE link to a Whoop 4.0 (service 61080001-…) or 5.0/MG (service fd4b0001-…, puffin envelope), using the hardware-verified protocol from NOOP (johnmiddleton12/my-whoop) for 4.0 and Rudra5417/whoop5-protocol (ZHOOP) for 5.0. The live stream (type 0x28, ~1 Hz) carries heart rate and one RR interval in milliseconds — SpO2 and skin temperature live in the band's stored history, not the live stream. Tap Sync history (5.0/MG only) to pull the store: it pauses the live stream, runs the offload chunk by chunk, and shows the latest skin temp. Absolute °C is a trend, not a calibrated reading. The direct link needs the band bonded to this phone: the strap holds one encrypted bond, created by pairing it in the official WHOOP app (unpair from inside that app first if it's bonded to another phone — removing it in iOS Settings isn't enough). Without the bond the hello write goes out unanswered. Whoop 3.0 and other straps keep using the standard heart-rate broadcast path, unchanged.

Red dots are detected R-peaks; orange dots are intervals more than 20% off their neighbours. They are flagged and left out of HRV, not diagnosed.
